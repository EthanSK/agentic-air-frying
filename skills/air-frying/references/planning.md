# Planning examples

## Two drawers, one load

**Prompt:** “I have chilled firm tofu, fresh broccoli and frozen potato wedges. Can I put everything in my two-drawer fryer and leave it alone until dinner?”

- Check the tofu and wedge packaging and ask about the model only if its layout, capacity, or finish-together function matters. Use the frozen wedges' own directions.
- Put the tofu and broccoli in drawer 1 if their cooking instructions and piece sizes allow the same program. Keep them in a loose single layer and give each room. If the broccoli needs much less time, give it a separate batch or side method rather than baking that mismatch into the plan.
- Put the wedges in drawer 2 and set it independently. Use the model's finish-together option if available. If the wedge packet calls for shaking, say so and give a no-shake alternative only if one is appropriate.
- Explain any differences in when to check the two drawers. Numbers depend on the exact products, portions, and fryer.

## One drawer or incompatible settings

Put compatible foods together only when they fit with room for airflow and can safely run at one setting. For foods with incompatible instructions or a load that fills the basket, offer separate batches and say which one to cook first. If another appliance is available, suggest a simple simultaneous option that suits that food.

## After the cook

When someone reports uneven browning, a soft finish, or dry food, ask what happened only where their observation leaves a material gap. Use the amount, cut size, placement, settings and result they supplied to propose one specific change for the next batch. Avoid turning one outcome into a preset for different food or equipment.

For cleaning, follow the appliance cleaning instructions once the removable parts cool. Give model-specific dishwasher or coating advice only when confirmed in its manual.

For food safety, use the cook's local authority. The [UK Food Standards Agency cooking guide](https://www.food.gov.uk/safety-hygiene/cooking-your-food) covers package directions, separation of raw and ready-to-eat food, and checking that food is cooked through.
